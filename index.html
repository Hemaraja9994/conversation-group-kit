A weekly circle where everyone gets a turn to talk

For retired teachers and other volunteers running a conversation group for people living with aphasia after stroke, or with hearing loss. Eight sessions, each with a warm-up, a main topic and things to bring. The aim is conversation and company, not therapy.

Sessions

Facilitator tips

  • Keep the group small: 4 to 8 members, in a circle, in a quiet room with good light.
  • Write the topic and key words in large letters on a board or chart paper.
  • Give everyone a turn, and wait. Silence is thinking time.
  • Offer choices, yes-or-no questions, pictures, gestures and writing as ways to answer.
  • Never speak for someone or finish their sentence unless they ask. Treat every member as the capable adult they are.
  • For members with hearing loss: one person speaks at a time, face the group, and repeat questions from others.
  • Note anyone who seems unwell, low in mood or newly struggling, and tell the coordinating therapist.

Based on the principles of Supported Conversation for Adults with Aphasia (Kagan et al., 2001) and community aphasia group practice.
